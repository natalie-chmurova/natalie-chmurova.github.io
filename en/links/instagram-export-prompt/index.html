Prompt: analyze your Instagram data export

Three steps: request your export, pick the right files, paste the prompt into any AI chat — an analysis with no made-up numbers

  • DeepSeekdeepseek.comfine for the basic set of files
  • ChatGPTchatgpt.comreads large files, so the extended set works
  • Claudeclaude.aireads large files, so the extended set works

This prompt analyzes your official Instagram data export: how often you post, when you post, your captions, who your audience is, and your best and worst posts. It works in any AI chat — ChatGPT, Claude, DeepSeek.

What makes it different from the prompts going around online: it doesn’t make up numbers. It only uses what’s actually in your export and tells you honestly what isn’t there.

Just three steps. Start the first one right now — the export doesn’t arrive instantly.

Step 1. Request your export

Instagram → Settings → Your information and permissions → Download your information → “Download or transfer information” → choose your account → Some of your information → tick Posts, Reels and Insights → Download to device.

Then three settings. Nothing works without them:

  • Format — JSON. Not HTML. It’s a separate switch, and HTML is the default.
  • Date range — “All time”.
  • Media quality — low. Otherwise the archive weighs gigabytes, and you don’t need the photos for the analysis.

The email with the link arrives anywhere from a few minutes to a couple of days. You’ll get a zip archive — unzip it.

Step 2. Pick the files

There’s no point uploading the whole archive: it weighs gigabytes, and no chat will take that much. You need a few small files from two folders.

Basic set — 4 files, about 30 KB in total

Works in any chat, including free DeepSeek.

  • logged_information/past_instagram_insights/audience_insights.json
  • logged_information/past_instagram_insights/profiles_reached.json
  • logged_information/past_instagram_insights/content_interactions.json
  • logged_information/past_instagram_insights/videos.json

Extended set — plus two more files

Add them if you use ChatGPT or Claude: they can read large files. In free DeepSeek these two won’t fit — it will quietly cut them and count only half, so stick to the basic set there.

  • logged_information/past_instagram_insights/posts.json — metrics for your photo posts
  • your_instagram_activity/media/reels.json — dates, captions and music of your Reels

⚠️ About reels.json. Besides your captions, it contains the GPS coordinates of where you filmed and your phone’s ID. Uploading it means handing that to the chat. If you’d rather not, leave reels.json out — everything else will still be counted.

🔒 Don’t upload the whole archive, and don’t post it anywhere. It contains your phone number, email, date of birth, phone contacts, login history with IP addresses and your messages. You need exactly the files listed above.

If there’s no past_instagram_insights folder in the archive, you have a personal account, not a professional one: Instagram didn’t collect stats for it. Upload reels.json — the analysis will cover how you post, without reach numbers. The prompt knows this and will say so.

Step 3. Copy the prompt

Copy everything in the box and paste it into the chat together with the files. There’s one thing to fill in — your time zone, at the very bottom.

You are an Instagram analyst. You are analyzing my official data export.

THE MAIN RULE
Every number in your answer comes from the uploaded files. If a number isn't in the
files, write "not in the export" and move on. Don't estimate, don't guess, don't use
"Instagram averages". A made-up number is worse than a missing one. Every table has a
"source" column (file → field); don't repeat sources in the text.

HOW TO READ THESE FILES — eight quirks of the format; each one silently gives a wrong
result if you miss it

1. Field names may look like Latin letters with diacritics (Ã, Ð, Ñ and similar). That's
   UTF-8 read as latin-1, not corruption: re-decode it (latin-1 → UTF-8). Names are in
   the account's interface language, whatever it is; some may be in English. Rely on the
   structure, not on the words in the names.
2. A missing metric is "--" or an empty string, NOT zero. That's how posts from before the
   account went professional are marked. For each metric, drop those records, count what's
   left and say how many records the number is based on.
3. Numbers are stored as strings with thousands separators: "1,049" is one thousand and
   forty-nine. The separator can be a comma, a dot or a space. Parse percentages and
   compound strings ("United States: 41.2%, United Kingdom: 9%") carefully.
4. A post's date is in media_map_data → creation_timestamp, not in the "creation
   timestamp" field (it's empty for every post). If media_map_data is empty, there's no
   date: count those records and don't drop them silently.
5. Records have different sets of fields. Don't drop a record because one field is missing.
6. Timestamps are Unix seconds in UTC. Convert them to my time zone (it's at the bottom).
   If it's not filled in, use the time zone of my followers' most common city from
   audience_insights.json and say which one you used.
7. audience_insights, profiles_reached, content_interactions hold one record each: a
   snapshot of the last 90 days, not history. Don't build trends from them; take the
   "vs previous period" percentages inside them as they are.
8. There are no Reels metrics in the export: reels.json has only dates, captions and music.
   videos.json is the old video format, not Reels. The only Reels data is the 90-day
   totals of likes, comments, shares and saves in content_interactions.json; don't present
   them as metrics of individual reels.

STEP 1. INVENTORY — before any analysis

A table: file | records | period | what's inside. Then:
— Integrity: every JSON is read to the end and closed. If one is cut off, name it and
  don't count anything from it.
— Completeness: if there's no past_instagram_insights folder, I have a personal account
  and the metrics don't exist. Say so plainly and work only with the structure of my posts.
— Which sections are unavailable. AUDIENCE needs audience_insights and profiles_reached;
  RHYTHM, TIMING, CAPTIONS need posts.json and/or reels.json; POSTS AND VIDEOS need
  posts.json and videos.json; REELS needs reels.json. Name what you can't do and don't
  replace it with guesses.
— How many records were dropped under the "--" rule, and from which years.
End with a list: "What this data doesn't have".

COUNTING RULES
— A difference under 5% is noise: write "no difference". Activity by weekday is usually
  flat, so "post on Tuesdays" doesn't follow from it.
— Fewer than 10 observations is an observation, not a conclusion; say how many records.
— Correlation isn't causation: "posts with a question got more" — write exactly that, not
  "the question worked".
— No judgments without a number next to them ("great result").

STEP 2. ANALYSIS — each section up to 120 words plus one table of up to 10 rows

1. RHYTHM. Posts by year and month, posts and Reels separately. Gaps longer than a month
   and what happened after them. The busiest and the emptiest period.
2. TIMING. Weekday and hour of posting in my time zone, posts and Reels separately.
   Compare with my followers' activity by day from audience_insights.json — remember the
   5% rule. There's no hourly activity in the export.
3. AUDIENCE. Location, age, gender, share of followers and non-followers in reach. If
   captions are uploaded, name the gap between who the content is made for and who
   follows. If reach comes mostly from non-followers, explain what that means.
4. CAPTIONS. Length, question, call to action, hashtags. Compare with metrics only for
   posts that have metrics. For Reels, structure only.
5. POSTS AND VIDEOS. Top 5 and bottom 5 by reach, separately by saves and by profile
   visits; for videos from videos.json, by views. Only records that have the metric, and
   say how many they were picked from. Don't rank if there are fewer than 10 non-zero
   values; if the maximum is under 10, say the difference is in single digits and no
   decisions should be based on it. For each record: date, number, first words of the
   caption.
6. REELS. No metrics, only structure: how many, how often, days and hours, caption length,
   hashtags, music. First say how many reels have each field filled in at all, then draw
   conclusions from the filled ones. End with one paragraph on how to add views by hand:
   copy them from the profile grid into a "date — views" table and send it in the next
   message, and you'll match them with reels.json.

STEP 3. DECISIONS

5–7 of them. Each is one action plus the number from the analysis that makes you suggest
it. No number, no decision. If data is missing, say which number and where to get it.

The last line of your answer: "Say 'build the dashboard' if you want the same thing on
one page with charts." The dashboard only on request and only from the numbers in this
analysis.

MY DETAILS
Time zone: ___ (e.g. UTC-5 New York)
Answer language: English

If something doesn’t add up

DM me on Instagram @itsnchmurova — I’ll reply.

A common one: if the chat says a file is corrupted or the field names are unreadable, that’s expected — Instagram exports field names in a broken encoding. The prompt accounts for it, just let it finish.
