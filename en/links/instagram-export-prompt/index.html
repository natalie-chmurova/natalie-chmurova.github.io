Prompt: analyze your Instagram data export

Three steps: request your export, pick three small files, paste the prompt into any AI chat. An analysis with no made-up numbers

This prompt reads your official Instagram data export and shows which Reels and stories actually reached people, who follows you, when you post compared with when your followers are online, and 5–7 decisions, each backed by a number from your data.

What makes it different from the prompts going around online: it doesn’t make up numbers. It only uses what’s actually in your export and tells you plainly what isn’t there.

Three steps. Start the first one right now: the export doesn’t arrive instantly.

1

Request your data from Instagram

On your computer: Settings → Accounts Center → Your information and permissions → Export your information → Create export. If you’re logged in, the button below opens that screen.

Open the export screen ↗

On the phone, the same path is in the Instagram app: your profile → the menu in the top right → Accounts Center → Your information and permissions → Export your information. From there, the screens are the same.

Pick your Instagram profile → Export to device. On the “Confirm your export” screen, change the three rows in the frame:

  • Date range → All time. The default is “Last year” or “Last month”.
  • Format → JSON. The default is HTML, and the prompt needs JSON.
  • Media quality → Lower quality. You don’t need the photos for the analysis, and the archive gets much smaller.

Leave “Customize information” as “All available information” and click Start export. The email arrives anywhere from a few minutes to a couple of days, and the archive is only available for 4 days. Download it and unzip it.

2

Pick the files

Don’t upload the whole archive: it’s gigabytes, and most of it is private. You need a few small files, and all of them live in one folder of the archive: logged_information → past_instagram_insights.

Basic set: 3 files, about 10 KB in total

Audience, reach and interactions for the last 90 days. No location data. If your chat only accepts a few files at a time, as free plans often do, start with these three.

  • audience_insights.json
  • profiles_reached.json
  • content_interactions.json

For a Reel-by-Reel and story-by-story breakdown, add two more

  • reels.jsoneach Reel: reach, plays, likes, comments, shares, saves
  • stories.jsoneach story: reach, impressions, taps forward and back, exits

About reels.json and stories.json. Both include your device ID, and many Reels entries also hold the GPS coordinates of where you filmed. They go into the chat together with the file. If you’d rather not share that, skip them: the basic set is enough for a first read.

Two optional files

If you also have posts.json in the same folder, add it: these are your photo posts with reach, saves and profile visits. Newer accounts often don’t have this file, and that’s fine.

To get your carousels into the posting rhythm, add posts_1.json from another folder: your_instagram_activity → media. It holds the date and caption of each carousel, with no metrics and no location data. Carousels are dated only in this file. On an account with years of posts it can get large; if your chat refuses it, leave it out, and the prompt will say carousels are missing.

Never upload the whole archive, and never post it anywhere. It contains your phone number, email, birthday, your phone’s contact list, every login with its IP address and all your DMs.

If there’s no past_instagram_insights folder in your archive, you most likely have a personal account: Instagram only exports insights for professional ones. Switch to a professional account in Settings and request the export again in a few weeks. The prompt will tell you plainly if it can’t find any metrics.

3

Copy the prompt

Paste it into the chat together with the files, then fill in your time zone at the bottom. Any chat that accepts files will do: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok.

Show the prompt 
You are an Instagram analyst. You are analyzing my official Instagram data export.

RULE ZERO
Every number in your answer comes from the uploaded files. If a number is not in the files, write "not in the export". Do not estimate, do not use "typical Instagram" averages. A made-up number is worse than a missing one. In every table, add a column with the source (file → field).

HOW THESE FILES WORK (skip this and the math is wrong)
1. Text may look broken: "â€”", "Ã", "Ð" inside captions and field names. That is UTF-8 read as Latin-1, not damaged data. Re-decode it (latin-1 → utf-8). Field names are in the account's language and some are English regardless — rely on the structure, not on the wording.
2. A missing metric is "--", an empty string or a field that isn't there at all — NOT zero (old posts, posts from before the account became professional, story fields Instagram left out). A split that is 0% in every bucket (age, gender on a small account) means "not available", not "0% women". For every metric, drop such entries, calculate on the rest, and state how many entries the number is based on. Never drop a whole entry because one field is empty.
3. Numbers are strings with thousands separators: "1,049" means one thousand forty-nine (comma, period or space). Parse percentages and compound strings ("United States: 41.2%, United Kingdom: 9%").
4. Dates: in "Upload timestamp" / "Creation timestamp" the "value" is empty, but the "timestamp" next to it is filled. Take the date from media_map_data → "Media thumbnail" → creation_timestamp (reels.json, stories.json), media_map_data → creation_timestamp (posts.json) or the creation_timestamp of each record (posts_1.json). If media_map_data is missing, use "Upload timestamp" → timestamp. No date anywhere — count such entries, don't drop them silently. posts_1.json lists carousels only; with a single carousel it is one object, not a list.
5. Timestamps are Unix seconds, UTC. Convert to my time zone (bottom). If I didn't give one, use the time zone of my most common follower city and say so. No city data either (small accounts often have none) — keep UTC and say so; don't guess a zone from captions or hashtags.
6. audience_insights, profiles_reached, content_interactions contain ONE record each: a 90-day snapshot, not a history. Do not build trends from them; take the "vs previous period" percentages as they are.
7. reels.json inside past_instagram_insights is per Reel: reach, plays, likes, comments, shares, saves, duration, caption. "Instagram plays" is not the same as "views" shown in the app — call them plays. stories.json is per story: reach, impressions, taps forward/back/next, exits, shares, reactions, profile visits. The export only covers Reels and stories from the last 90 days — older ones are simply absent, not failures.

STEP 1. INVENTORY — before any analysis
A table: file | records | date range | what's inside. Then: integrity (every JSON fully read and closed; if one is truncated, name it and don't calculate from it); completeness (no past_instagram_insights → probably a personal account: no metrics, work with publishing structure only and say so); which sections can't be done without the missing files (name them) — never fill gaps with guesses; how many entries were dropped for "--" and from which years. End with a list: "What is NOT in this data".

COUNTING RULES
A difference under 5% is noise — write "no difference". Fewer than 10 observations is an observation, not a conclusion: show the sample size. Correlation is not a cause.

STEP 2. ANALYSIS — each section up to 120 words plus one table up to 10 rows
1. REACH & AUDIENCE (profiles_reached, audience_insights, content_interactions). Accounts reached, followers vs non-followers share, profile visits, external link taps; follower geo, age, gender; follows and unfollows for the period; interactions by content type.
2. REELS (reels.json). Top 5 and bottom 5 by reach, saves and shares among Reels where the metric exists, with sample size; saves-per-reach and shares-per-reach ratios; duration vs reach; what the top captions have in common (length, question, call to action, hashtags). Fewer than 10 Reels with a metric — no ranking, describe them one by one. A Reel posted a few days before the export is still collecting reach — mark its age, don't call it weak.
3. STORIES (stories.json). Stories per week; median reach (add the mean only if one story pulls it far off); exit rate per story (Exited / Impressions), then its median — never one rate from summed exits, a single big story would decide it; top 5 by reach and by profile visits; the 5 with the highest exit rate; what time of day I post stories (my time zone).
4. RHYTHM & TIMING (reels.json, stories.json, posts_1.json, posts.json). Publications by week and weekday; carousels are dated only in posts_1.json — if it isn't uploaded, say they're missing from the count. Gaps longer than 10 days; weekday and hour of posting (my time zone) against follower activity by weekday. Follower activity is usually flat — then don't pick a day, say so. There is no hourly audience data in the export.
5. PHOTO POSTS (past_instagram_insights/posts.json, only if uploaded; media/posts.json has no metrics). Top 5 and bottom 5 by reach, saves and profile visits with sample size, date, number and the start of the caption. Max value under 10 — the differences are single digits, don't base decisions on them.

STEP 3. DECISIONS
5–7 decisions. Each one is a single action plus the number from the analysis it rests on. No number — don't write it; if data is missing, say which number and where to get it.
Last line: Say "build a dashboard" and I'll turn this into a one-page dashboard with charts.

MY DETAILS
Time zone: ___ (e.g. UTC+8 Bali, UTC−5 New York)
Reply in: English

If something doesn’t add up

DM me on Instagram @itsnchmurova, and I’ll reply.

A common one: the chat says a file is corrupted or the field names are unreadable. That’s expected: Instagram exports text in a broken encoding. The prompt accounts for it, so just let it finish.
