How to write a good prompt

The three parts AI actually needs — instead of “you are an expert with 300 years of experience”

A ready-made prompt is a checklist that keeps you from forgetting something, not a magic spell you copy word for word. Good results don’t come from “you are an expert with 300 years of experience”. They come from explaining clearly what you need.

Three parts

  1. Context. Who you are, what you sell or do, and who it’s for.
  2. A specific task. What exactly to write, calculate or put together — one action.
  3. What a good result looks like. Length, tone, and what it shouldn’t include.

Example

I sell an online course for beginner designers. My audience is afraid they have no talent. Write a short Instagram caption that eases this fear and invites them to a free lesson. No pressure, no big promises, and no “change your life”.

No “marketer with ten years of experience” needed here: the AI already has the context, the task and the criteria.

And skip the perfect prompt. Give it context, get a first draft, then improve it in the chat.
