How to edit video with AI★

My talking videos are edited by Claude: it cuts pauses and repeats, speeds them up, adds subtitles and mixes the sound. My part is one evening per video, with a laptop or a phone: I watch the draft and write my notes. I’m not an editor, and I have never once opened an editing app. Below: what this is made of, how it works, and what you can do today. I also help people set this up for themselves, more on that at the end.

1

What you get

The main point is not nice inserts or livelier music. The main point is that the AI takes over the boring part: pauses, looking away, subtitles, sound. That is what saves my evenings.

What it does. It edits a talking video by its text. The AI transcribes the speech word by word, cuts pauses, slips and repeats, speeds it up, adds subtitles and mixes the sound. A five-minute recording where I say every phrase several times becomes a one-minute video. Any extra inserts (texts, labels, pictures) are separate work on top of this.

How much time. One evening per video, and my part of it is watching the draft and giving notes. The setup took a few days. But it never really ends: after every video I add rules, and this has been going on for over a month now. So no promises of “one prompt, ten minutes”. That only works if you don’t count the notes.

Footnote

Everyone has their own idea of “good enough” and “I like it”. You can build something like this in two hours, and that is true. But getting to a result that you like takes time.

What the AI won’t do for you

It cuts, speeds up, draws and fixes its own mistakes. Four things stay with me:

  • deciding what to film;
  • noticing that it made a mistake;
  • understanding what matters to the people in the frame;
  • being responsible for the result: my name is under the video.

Each of these became a rule for it. Which rules exactly depends on how you film and how you speak, so there is no universal list.

What it’s made of

I wrote nothing on this list from scratch. It all already exists. My work was to explain to the AI how it should all work together, and how I want my videos cut.

An AI agent. An AI that lives not in a browser but on your computer: it reads files, runs programs and tells you what it did. An ordinary chat can advise you how to edit, but it can’t reach your file. That is the whole difference.

I edit in Claude Code from Anthropic. Codex from OpenAI can do the same: I’ve edited the same video with both. There are other agents too, for example Gemini CLI from Google. I haven’t tested them for editing, but they work the same way, and everything below applies to them too.

Three free programs. One cuts video and mixes the sound (ffmpeg). The second transcribes speech word by word (Whisper). The third draws graphics over my footage (Remotion). The AI writes the commands for them. I don’t need to know them.

A rules file. The most important part. A plain text file that the AI reads at the start of every session: how to cut me, speed me up and subtitle me. In essence, this is my editor. The rest are tools.

Step-by-step instructions. I have 12 folders of them by now (I started with five 😂) on how the AI goes through the footage and builds the video. They are written for my files and my mistakes, and rewritten after every video. In other hands they won’t work, which is why they are not here.

This is a poll, not a download button: I’m counting how many people need them. If enough do, I’ll make a version that works for people other than me.

By the way, about sound

I record sound on an Apple Watch. I measured it: on the watch, my voice is 43 dB above the noise; on a phone in the same room, only 16 dB. The best microphone in the house was on my wrist.

3

How it works

I film a week of videos in one block. I read the text from my phone or laptop, so I look away a lot and start phrases over. By the rules, all of that goes. I put the file in a folder and write one line.

What I write

Edit it.

A draft comes back. I watch it on my phone and write notes by piece numbers (ideally), not with words like “make it more dynamic”. But often I do write in words, and Claude understands me anyway :) It rebuilds the video and sends only what changed. Two or three rounds of notes, and the video is ready. How the build works inside, I leave out: it depends entirely on the footage, and yours will be different.

A few of my rules:

  • There is always speech in the frame. Pauses longer than half a second are cut. Shorter ones stay: that is breathing.
  • If I turned to my phone, cut it together with the silence.
  • The picture jumping at the cuts is not a problem. We cut by text.
  • Speed up by 8%, but so that you can’t hear it.
  • Put nothing on the face: no labels, no subtitles, no pictures.

My other rules appeared after specific videos, after mistakes the AI made on my footage in particular. They are no longer universal. This is the tuning for yourself, and it continues with every video.

4

Where to start if you want the same

Step 1. Write your rules. No computer needed. Collect references (videos whose editing you like) and dictate into an ordinary chat everything you want from editing: what to cut, what to keep, the speed, the subtitles. If nothing else works, sit down and write it yourself, half a page. Without this, any AI edits “like everyone”, not like you. Below are questions worth answering, with example answers.

QuestionExample answers
What to always cut?Pauses longer than half a second, looking away at the phone, slips and repeats. From several takes: keep the last one, or choose yourself.
What speed?8% faster, but so that you can’t hear it. Or no speed-up, if you already speak fast.
What subtitles?Two words at a time, in the center of the frame. Or one word at a time, or no subtitles at all.
What must never be covered in the frame?The face: labels, inserts and subtitles live around it. Or the lower third is always free for text.
How does the video start and end?If the first phrase hooks, start with it, with no fade-in; if there is a gesture at the end, don’t cut it. Or a smooth start and a musical tail.

Step 2. Install the agent. Here you need a computer. Installation guides: Claude Code at code.claude.com/docs, Codex at developers.openai.com/codex. For the other programs, ask the AI itself to install them. It manages. You can start with the ready-made Remotion skill. It is public (remotion.dev/docs/ai/skills) and teaches the agent to draw graphics over video. Copy the line and give it to the agent:

npx skills add remotion-dev/skills

Step 3. The first task: one boring thing. Start small. Don’t ask right away for a finished, polished video with all the inserts: that is a mistake. Start with pauses and repeats. That is the biggest time saver. How to set the task so it doesn’t cut too much is below, in “Where people get stuck”.

Step 4. Add one line at a time. Every time you fix something by hand, add a rule to the file. After a few videos, you have your own editor.

You can do step 1 tonight. Steps 2–4 are the terminal or Cowork, the installation and the first notes, which is where people stumble. You can go through them on your own, or in one meeting with me. At the end I explain how.

Where people get stuck

Four places where I stumbled. None of them is about buttons. All of them are about how the AI understands video. Two are solved by a rule I give here. For the other two, the cause is technical and depends on what you cut with, and that’s where we start in the meeting.

It says “cut”, but it’s still in the video

symptomYou ask it to remove the pause at a certain second, it answers “done”, but the piece next to it was cut.
what to doDon’t take “done” on trust: ask for the frame before and after the cut. The cause is technical; the one-line fix is what we do in the meeting.

The gesture at the end disappeared

symptomThe video ends on the last word. The smile, the wave, the kiss are cut.
ruleCheck the end of the video with your eyes, not by the speech transcript: where there are no words, the AI decides the video is over. For me it is one line in the file, and the gesture no longer disappears.

The note misses the target

symptomYou describe the problem in words, it changes something and sends it back, and the problem is still there. And so on, in circles.
ruleGive notes by piece numbers and seconds, not with words like “more dynamic”. And ask it to send only what changed: then you see at once whether the note landed.

The frame for checking is the wrong one

symptomYou ask it to show the frame at the twelfth second, and it sends a moment next to it.
causeTechnical: with fast seeking, it jumps to a nearby frame, not the one you named. It is fixed with one line, but the line depends on the tool.

My other rules appeared the same way: a symptom, a cause, a line in the file. That is what we work on in the meeting, on your footage, not mine.

What it costs

The agent: a subscription. The free plan is not enough for Claude Code. The $20-a-month plan is enough if you build the setup once and don’t edit many videos in a row. Mine is $100, only because I do a lot of other things in it in parallel.

Codex is included in a ChatGPT subscription and is available even on the free plan, but with a smaller limit. I haven’t measured whether it is enough for editing. The agent is the only real expense. Prices and limits change, so check the sites.

The other programs: zero. They are free and open source, and you install them once.

Time: a few days of setup. After that, a video takes an evening, of which yours is the watching and the notes, plus one line in the rules file after each.

What I’m left with

The main thing is the rules file: my taste, written in words so that someone else can carry it out. Before, I couldn’t explain to an editor what I wanted. Now the explanation sits in a folder and works every week.
